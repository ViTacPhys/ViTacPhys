Abstract

Object physical properties such as mass, stiffness, and friction directly determine how a robot should grasp, support, and manipulate an object, yet they are often hidden from static visual observations and difficult to estimate before contact. Humans naturally combine visual priors with dense tactile feedback during interaction to infer these properties and adapt their actions. Motivated by this capability, we introduce H2Touch, a human visual-tactile manipulation dataset collected with a wearable sensing system that synchronizes wrist-mounted RGB observations and fingertip piezoresistive tactile maps. H2Touch covers 60 everyday objects from 7 categories with explicit annotations for mass, structural stiffness, and static friction coefficient. Based on this dataset, we propose a temporal multimodal physical-property predictor that fuses RGB frames, tactile pressure maps, visual optical flow, tactile flow, and language-derived semantic priors. We further study human-to-robot transfer by adapting the predictor to a dexterous robot and using its outputs as structured property tokens for an ACT-style adaptive grasping policy. Experiments show that the predictor generalizes to unseen objects, transfers to robot observations with limited robot data, and improves real-robot grasp success and force adaptation, especially for visually similar out-of-distribution objects with different physical properties.

Method

ViTacPhys has two stages: a visual-tactile property predictor and a property-conditioned ACT policy.

Rendering figure…

Property predictor. RGB, tactile, and motion-flow streams are encoded by two-stream temporal encoders (ResNet-18 + GRU) and fused with a VLM-distilled semantic prior through gated cross-attention to predict mass, stiffness, and friction.

Rendering figure…

Property-conditioned ACT policy. After contact, the predicted properties are discretized into voted property tokens and injected with wrist RGB and proprioception, letting the policy adapt grasp force, finger closure, and anti-slip behavior.

Data Collection

A wearable glove with three fingertip tactile arrays and a wrist-mounted RGB camera, synchronized with motion capture at 30 Hz, records humans grasping, lifting, and shaking everyday objects.

Rendering figure…

Acquisition setup. Wrist RGB, fingertip tactile maps, and motion-capture markers are aligned to match the real robot's sensing layout. Mass, stiffness, and friction are then labeled with a precision scale, force–displacement fitting, and an inclined-plane test.

Experimental Platform

Rendering figure…

A 7-DoF manipulator with a direct-drive 6-DoF dexterous hand; fingertips carry piezoresistive tactile arrays matching the human glove. Demonstrations are collected by teleoperation with a Quantum Manus glove — 10 demos for each of 50 objects.
